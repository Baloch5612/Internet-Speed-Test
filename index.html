A Combined Upload and Download Speed Test

Most free speed test tools only check your download speed, leaving your upload speed as a guess. This tool runs a full upload and download speed test in one pass, along with ping, so you get the complete picture of your connection in a single run.

Your network performance moves based on connection latency, server bandwidth capacities, and regional ISP routing protocols. This tool measures both download and upload capacity by timing real data transfers to and from your device.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this check both upload and download speed?

Yes. Press Run Test once and it measures your ping, then your download speed, then your upload speed, displaying all three results separately — so you don't need a second tool to check upload speed or latency.

Why does ping matter, not just download speed?

Ping (latency) measures how quickly your connection responds, not how much data it can move. It matters most for gaming, video calls, and anything real-time — a connection can have fast download speeds but still feel laggy if ping is high.

What do the Streaming, Gaming, and Video Call ratings mean?

After a test completes, this tool estimates how well your connection suits three common activities based on your ping, download, and upload results. These are general estimates, not official benchmarks, since actual performance also depends on the specific service and device you're using.

What is a good internet download speed?

A speed between 25 Mbps and 50 Mbps is ideal for standard high-definition streaming, remote work, and browsing. For heavy 4K streaming or video calls on multiple devices at once, connection levels above 100 Mbps are recommended.

Why is my upload speed lower than my download speed?

Most home broadband connections are asymmetric by design, since typical household usage downloads far more than it uploads. Cable and DSL connections usually show the biggest gap; fiber connections tend to be closer to symmetric.

How does this speed test work?

The tool downloads a file of known size from a nearby test server and times how long the transfer takes to calculate your download speed, then uploads a file the same way to measure your upload speed. Both results are shown live on the gauge as the test runs.
